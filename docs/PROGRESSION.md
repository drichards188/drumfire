# Research, upgrades and the special missions

Germany has one career and one research tree: the guns of 1914 to 1918 and of the Second World War together, as a War Thunder nation. Britain (the 1917 game) is hidden from the menu for now; its tree is kept for when it comes back.

You start with two batteries, the **7.7 cm FK 16** field guns and the **15 cm sFH 13** heavy field howitzers, two guns of four each and no upgrades, and fight the **open battle of 1918** on the ridge with the batteries you have. Your first battle, at level 1, is fought with those four guns, loaded and ready; the basic training is where you try the late-war guns first. Every battle pays, won or lost. The pay goes into two purses:

- **Research points (RP)** go to the battery you are researching, picked on the Research screen. There is always one while anything is left; when it is done, the rest of the RP goes on to the next one in its line (or the cheapest open one).
- **Battery points**, per battery: each battery earns its own from its own fire, and spends them on its own upgrades, whenever you like.

## The tree

Seven lines, one type each, and each runs from the guns of 1914 to 1918 to those of the Second World War. A line is researched in order, and lines never cross.

**Ranks are eras, as in War Thunder.** Ranks I and II are the guns of 1914 to 1918 (I field and medium, II heavy and super-heavy), ranks III and IV those of 1939 to 1945 (III field, medium and rockets, IV heavy, super-heavy and the V-weapons). The price is the battery's power, whatever its era: a WW2 field howitzer is cheap, the WW1 siege guns are hard to get.

**A rank opens with the rank before it.** Rank I is open from the start. Each rank above opens once you have enough batteries of the rank before it, from any line:

- **Rank II** (1914 to 1918, heavy) with 3 of rank I. Your FK 16 and sFH 13 count, so the K 17, the one rank I battery to research, opens it: about your first battle's pay.
- **Rank III** (1939 to 1945) with 3 of rank II. The cheaper heavies will do: you can go on to the second war without the siege guns, but from threat level 6 the strongpoints only break under 28 cm shells and more, so a cheap lineup stalls there.
- **Rank IV** (1939 to 1945, heavy) with 4 of rank III.

On the Research screen a rank still shut shows how many it has of how many it needs (2/3) under its number; hover a rank for its era.

| Line | Batteries in order (rank, RP) |
| --- | --- |
| Field artillery | 7.7 cm FK 16 (I, yours) → 15 cm sFH 13 (I, yours) → 10.5 cm leFH 18 (III, 600) → 15 cm sFH 18 (III, 1,100) → Hummel (IV, 2,000) |
| Long-range guns | 10 cm K 17 (I, 350) → 15 cm K 16 (II, 700) → 17 cm K 18 (III, 1,100) → 21 cm K 38 (IV, 2,000) |
| Mortars | 21 cm Mörser 16 (II, 900) → 21 cm Mörser 18 (III, 1,300) |
| Siege guns | 30.5 cm Beta-Gerät (II, 2,000) → 42 cm Bertha (II, 2,400) → Karl-Gerät (IV, 4,000) |
| Railway guns | 38 cm Langer Max (II, 2,400) → Gustav (IV, 5,000) |
| Off-map support | Gotha strike (II, 2,000) → V-2 (IV, 4,500) |
| Rockets | Nebelwerfer 41 (III, 450) → Nebelwerfer 42 (III, 1,200) |

**Twenty batteries since 2026-11-26** (34,000 RP to the end of the tree). Each one is a step up on the battery before it in its line, in something the open battle asks for. The ranks hold 3, 6, 6 and 5.

What the new batteries bring (2026-11-01):

- **FK 16.** The quickest gun in the tree: shrapnel over infantry, the Sperrfeuer and the Feuerwalze. Its light shell does nothing to tanks or concrete.
- **Your first batteries: the FK 16 and the sFH 13** (since 2026-11-26; the Ringkanone C/92 from 2026-11-01, the K 17 before), two guns of four each. The field guns are quick and light, the howitzers' 42 kg shell breaks gun pits and dumps. The K 17 is the first thing to research. A career begun with the K 17 keeps it.
- **Taken out (2026-11-26): the ramp guns**, the 15 cm Ringkanone C/92, the 15 cm sFH 93 and the 21 cm Mörser 99, old guns with no recoil system that ran up their ramps and were laid afresh every round. Nothing you earned is lost: the RP put into the sFH 93 and the Mörser 99 come back, and so do those put into the FK 16 and the sFH 13 (yours from the start now); the C/92's guns, parts and battery points go to the sFH 13, which also takes its slot in your lineup; the sFH 93's battery points, and what its upgrades cost, go to the sFH 13, the Mörser 99's to the Mörser 16; and a second gun you bought for the FK 16 or the sFH 13, now free, comes back as points.
- **Taken out (2026-11-26): the batteries that were no step up** on the one before them in their line: the schweres Wurfgerät 40 (its two kilometres reached nothing but posts made for it), the 35.5 cm H M1, the K 12, the Kurze Bruno and the K5 (the Langer Max does their work better), and the Wespe (the leFH 18's gun after the sFH 18). **The Gamma is merged into Bertha**: Bertha takes its place after the Beta-Gerät and its price, 2,400 RP; it is quicker, carries more shells and reaches every strongpoint. **The Paris Gun and the V-1** leave the tree but keep their own missions, as they were. Nothing you earned is lost: the RP put into a cut battery come back; its battery points, and what its upgrades cost, go to the battery that follows it (the Wespe's to the Hummel, the H M1's to the Mörser 18, the railway guns' and the Paris Gun's to the Gustav, the Wurfgerät's to the Nebelwerfer 42, the V-1's to the V-2); a career that had the Gamma has Bertha, with the Gamma's guns, parts and place in the lineup (its RP come back if it had Bertha too, and so do the 400 RP Bertha cost over its new price).
- **Taken out (2026-11-02): the 25 cm schwerer Minenwerfer.** A kilometre's reach from behind our front trench left it two targets on the whole ridge. A career that researched it gets its 300 RP back.
- **Beta-Gerät.** The Gamma's flow on a smaller bed: T relays the bed past its arc.
- **K 16, K 38.** Long guns: counter-battery and the depots behind the British line. The K 16 reaches about 24 km, the first long gun on the deep targets.
- **Hummel.** Self-propelled: T drives the battery to its second position. The enemy loses it on the way, but the guns are out of action for a couple of minutes and the new position needs ranging. An auto crew moves by itself when it is found.
- **Gotha strike.** Three bombers cross the map from the east at 1,800 m and each drops a stick of four 100 kg bombs across the point you gave.

- **Off-map support.** The Gotha strike and the V-2 fight in the open battle from off the map: you pick one, give it a target, and it fires in its own time, then takes long to be ready again. The V-2 is the step up: one rocket in under four minutes silences a field battery or burns a dump, and two break up a tank laager, which the Gotha's bombs cannot. The V-2's own launch mission stays, open to all, and so do the V-1's and the Paris Gun's.
- **The other battles.** The special missions are open to all, as before. The WW2 open battle is out of the menu for now: the higher threat levels are to bring the career onto its map.
- **Old saves.** A career keeps every battery it has researched that is still in the tree (the cut ones are carried over, above). Owning a later battery never blocks you.
- **Growing the tree further.** Ideas per line: the 24 cm K 3; the leFH 16 and a leFH 18/40 folder; the 21 cm Mörser 10; the Wurfrahmen 40; Stuka strikes off the map (as the Gotha's WW2 counterpart).

## Your lineup

As in War Thunder's crew slots, at most **5 batteries on the map and 1 off it** take the field, in the order of their slots: slot 1 is key 1 and the first tab in battle. On the Research screen, click a battery you have and **Take into battle**: the slots it can take light up (an empty one, or one whose battery it replaces) and a click puts it there; **Change slot** swaps a battery of the lineup with another, and dragging a battery from the tree, or one slot onto another, does the same. The Hangar's bar along the bottom holds only the lineup's slots, each with a swap (⇄) that opens the bench (the batteries you have outside the lineup, on the map or off it as the slot is), where picking one puts it in that slot or the slot is left empty; an empty slot opens the bench at once, and "Leave out of battle" beside the battery empties its slot. Until you choose, the lineup is the best battery of each line (the first five lines', as there are five slots), then more field artillery (the Sperrfeuer's guns), then the heaviest; off the map the dearest. Dev mode opens every battery to the lineup, at its best, but the slots stay the same.

## Threat levels

The career's open battle is played at a **threat level** from 1 to 10 (as Helldivers 2's difficulties), picked in the battle chooser beside To battle. Level 1 is a quiet morning your two starting batteries can carry; level 10 asks everything of a full lineup at its best. A level sets the battle, never your own guns: its orders, the attacks and their size, the counter-battery fire, the aircraft, the kinds of target, the deadlines, and its pay (×1 up to ×4).

**The climb** (since 2026-11-26). The early levels are short and pay quickly; from the middle up a battle takes about a quarter of an hour whatever the level. What gets harder is the British fire on your guns and what the objectives ask, never the number of missions:

| Levels | What they are for | About |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 and 2 | the first session: a quick win, the loop learned in play | 4 to 7 min |
| 3 and 4 | the first hour: survive their fire and hit back | 9 to 11 min |
| 5 to 7 | the full tension: X-Zeit, concrete, two-step objectives | 13 to 16 min |
| 8 to 10 | a lineup that owns the tree: long holds under fire, two modifiers | 18 to 20 min |

- **The British guns answer by level.** Their 18-pounders from level 1, the 60-pounders and the 6-inch from level 4, the 9.2-inch from 5, the 15-inch from 7 and the 18-inch railway gun from 8. On the higher levels they need fewer ranging rounds before they fire for effect, more batteries fire at once, and their camouflage is better, so your rangers find them more slowly. From level 9 they shift after firing.
- **New at each level.** The level card says what the level brings that the one below did not ("New: ...") and how long it takes.

- **A mission, not a count.** Each battle deals one generated mission fitted to the level and your lineup. Its shape is one of four: counter-battery, their supply, their tanks or their command. It has main objectives and optional secondaries, each a named target on the map, such as "Blow up both ammunition dumps" or "Silence the 9.2-inch section". The menu's level card and the loading screen show it before battle. See "Missions" below.
- **It ends on its objectives, never on the clock.** When every main objective is done you end the battle when ready, or call X-Zeit if the mission has it. Too many failed fire missions on the main objectives lose (6 allowed at level 1, down to 3 from level 8). So do two sectors lost, or every gun.
- **All open:** every level can be played from the start, in any order.
- **Strongpoints (levels 6 to 10, with X-Zeit):** concrete strongpoints in the lanes of our X-Zeit assault, a secondary objective, two at levels 6 and 7, three at 8 and 9, four at 10. Only shells of 28 cm and more break them: the Langer Max, the Gustav, the Beta-Gerät, Bertha and the Karl, each of them able to reach every one (since 2026-11-26 Bertha's short range and the Gustav's position no longer leave any out). Lighter shells do nothing to them. Each one still standing at X-Zeit fires on our men in its lane, whatever the Feuerwalze does. Their missions open after the registration, by day and by night, with no clock: one at a time for each battery that can take them. So from level 6 one of the five slots wants a super-heavy, which costs a field battery's Sperrfeuer; the share of men the assault needs is a little lower there to make up for it.
- **Modifiers:** from level 5 one, from level 8 two, rolled for each battle and shown before it: ground mist, enemy air superiority, a shell shortage, enemy sound ranging, poor intelligence, rain.
- **The card** for each level shows what is new there, the mission dealt, its length, the attacks and the pay (with the first win's bonus until you have it), the pressure the level brings and whether your lineup fits it, or outclasses it (the pay is cut, below). Levels you have won are ticked. With no level picked, the chooser opens on the level your lineup suits and follows it as the lineup grows.

## Missions

The open battle at a threat level is one generated mission (`src/operation.js`, built after a game-design research pass on Helldivers 2, Deep Rock Galactic, Into the Breach and XCOM 2). The same seed deals the same mission in the menu, the loading screen and the battle.

- **A shape.** Each mission is one of four archetypes: **Gegenbatterie** (silence their batteries), **Nachschub** (their dumps and depots), **Tankabwehr** (break up their tank laagers) and **Führung** (their HQs and the shelters of their reserves). The shape decides which sites matter today.
- **Main objectives** are named targets: 1 at levels 1 to 3, 2 at levels 4 to 8, 3 at levels 9 and 10. On the higher levels a main names two or three targets of its kind. From level 4 one main may be reported during the battle, about the first attack. On levels 1 to 3 a main is always one your lineup can finish quickly. Mains never fail on a clock: an observer's window may close, then the mission comes again and the failure counts. Every objective's fire mission is out as soon as the objective is (since 2026-11-26; they used to open one at a time).
- **Holds.** "Keep Hellfire Corner under fire for 3:00": the clock runs while your bursts land near the place and waits when they stop, so it never fails, but it needs steady fire from one battery or several in turn while their guns range in on yours. A hold at the crossroads or the railhead holds their supply up; one on an attack's assembly area thins the attacks. Levels 1 and 2 have a short one as their secondary (level 1's a minute on Hellfire Corner, a job for the quick FK 16); at levels 3 to 7 one mission in two has a hold as a main; from level 8 every mission has a long one.
- **Two-step objectives** (from level 7). "Find and silence the battery, then burn its ammunition dump": a British battery your rangers have not placed yet, which shells your guns early on. Its fire mission comes once its rounds have given it away (or after five minutes, on a rough fix). Silence it and its dump is reported: burn that too.
- **Secondaries** are optional: 1 at levels 1 to 4 (at levels 1 and 2 a short hold), 2 at levels 5 to 8, 3 at levels 9 and 10. Two are known before battle; the rest are reported during it. Each one changes the battle: a dump gone slows their counter-battery fire, the HQ or a shelter gone thins their attacks, a laager broken up keeps its tanks out of the next attack, a train or convoy wrecked holds their supply up. Two are timed: a laager before its attack, and the trains and convoys while they run.
- **Only what you can do.** Mains are dealt only where your lineup is ready for them; a secondary may be a stretch (`capability.js`).
- **Attacks are pressure, not objectives.** None at level 1, one at levels 2 to 4, two at 5 to 7, three from 8. They come on the day's clock in rolled sectors whatever you do, spread over the level's length; finish quickly and you face fewer.
- **Their counter-battery fire** ranges in: a British battery walks its rounds onto one of your guns, then fires for effect. The radio and the battery's tab say how close they are. Take cover (U), switch to another battery, or shell theirs: your rangers place it from the rounds it fires, and a Gegenbatterie mission comes. A battery that falls silent under their fire for effect gets only the first salvo: they take it for knocked out. Crews on auto (while you serve a gun) take cover by themselves, not always in time.
- **You call the end.** When every main is done: "Mission accomplished". Finish what secondaries you will, then end the battle (K, or the button under the objectives), or call **X-Zeit**, the finale, when the mission has it (one in two missions at level 5, four in five at level 10). Their counter-battery fire grows 20% a minute meanwhile. A lineup all on auto ends it two minutes after the last objective moved.
- **Moments.** One at a time, never during an attack, the battle offers an objective of the moment (△ on the HUD, with its timer); each pays as a secondary but has no part in the full clear:
  - **The duel** (from level 3): a British battery has one of your batteries under its fire. Silence it within five minutes, or let your battery fall quiet; the HUD shows how far off their rounds fall, red when they are close.
  - **Counter-preparation:** before every attack its men mass in their front line for three minutes. Put a quarter of them down and the attack is called off.
  - **Targets of opportunity:** by day, every few minutes, a relief column halts on a road behind their line for three minutes. Put a third of it down (shrapnel) and it scatters.
- **The kite balloon** (a secondary from level 3): while it is up their counter-battery fire finds your guns sooner. Its fire mission is on the winch lorry on the ground: every near miss has the crew haul the balloon down for a few minutes, and the winch wrecked brings it down for good.
- **The railway-gun hunt** (a secondary from level 5 that pays double): the British 18-inch railway howitzer comes up to fire a few rounds, then withdraws out of sight. Each round it fires firms the sound rangers' fix; lay a battery on the fix and catch it when it fires again.
- **Medals** on the end card: no gun lost, and not one of theirs in our trenches.
- **The HUD** lists the objectives under the unit's name: squares for the mains, diamonds for the secondaries, progress and the failures allowed. The board rings each objective's target, and the missions list marks an objective's fire missions.
- The other battles (the WW2 sandbox, the special weapons' operations, the campaign) keep Easy, Normal and Hard.

## Upgrades

**Every battery starts with one gun** (the FK 16 and the sFH 13 you start with have two of their four). Its other guns come one at a time, into its empty gun pits. Each battery also has seven parts with three levels each:

- **Shells**: +12%, +25%, +40% damage. In the hangar the shells beside each gun show it: a fresh coat and a green band, then a white band and a taller fuze, then a pointed ballistic cap.
- **Barrel**: 15%, 28%, 40% tighter fall of shot, and less barrel wear.
- **Breech and loading gear**: 10%, 19%, 28% faster loading.
- **Sights and survey**: the first round lands 15%, 30%, 45% closer, and the guns lay faster.
- **Camouflage**: 12%, 24%, 36% less flash and noise for the enemy to locate.
- **Ammunition supply**: +20%, +40%, +60% shells in hand and faster resupply.
- **Relief crews**: +25%, +50%, +75% men at each gun, to take turns at the heavy work so the crew stays fresh (fatigue itself is not modelled). In the game the extra men are reserves, so a gun slows only when casualties take it below its drill's number, and the fresher hands load 3%, 6%, 10% faster. They stand in the hangar's ranks.

You buy them in any order with the battery's own points. Costs rise with the level and the battery's weight (field guns lightest, then heavy, super-heavy and the giants; not its rank, which is its era): a field gun's part costs 80, 170 and 300, and the second gun 120.

**The top level of every part waits for a hard win** (since 2026-11-26, as Helldivers 2's super samples): level III of shells, barrel, breech, sights, camouflage, supply and relief crews opens once you have won a battle at threat level 6 or higher. Until then the modifications panel says "Win a level 6 battle" in place of the Upgrade button (its cost on hover). More guns are never gated. A part already at level III stays there, and a level 6 battle played but lost opens nothing. Dev mode and Unlimited points open it.

## What a battle pays

Rewards come **as you earn them**, as in War Thunder and Enlisted: each one shows in the reward feed on the right of the 3D view (what it was, the battery that did it, the research points), with the battle's running total above it, and goes into your career at once.

- **Kills.** A target's score is paid half as its parts are knocked out (a gun of a battery, a tank of a laager, a lorry of a convoy, a stack of a dump) and half when it is destroyed.
- **Enemy casualties:** half a point for each of the enemy's men a burst kills.
- **Hits:** a quarter point for every point of damage, gathered into one line per target.
- **Missions:** a mission done shares its points among the batteries that fired on it, by rounds.
- **Objectives** (a threat level's mission): 100 for each main objective, 40 for each secondary, 80 more for every secondary (the full clear), 25 for each medal; the victory then pays 250 instead of 400. They count into the research points at the end, or when you leave.
- **RP** are the batteries' total, plus 4 for every minute in action and 400 for a battle won, paid at the end. Each battery keeps its own share as battery points.
- **The threat level** scales it all (×1 at level 1 to ×4 at level 10); the other battles' difficulty does (Easy 0.8, Normal 1, Hard 1.35).
- **The first win of each level** pays its victory again, once, straight to your research: 250 RP at levels 1 and 2, then 300, 400, 450, 500, 650, 750, 900, and 1,000 at level 10 (5,450 for all ten). The level card shows it ("First win: +N RP") until you have it, the end card's Next button names the next level's, and the summary lists it. It is never cut.
- **Outclassing the front pays less.** A lineup far above a level earns less there, so climbing pays and farming the quiet fronts does not: full pay down to two levels below the level your lineup suits, ×0.75 three below, ×0.5 four or more below. The level card warns ("Your lineup outclasses this front: pay ×0.75"), and the summary notes the cut. It touches everything the battle pays, research points and battery points, but never the first win's bonus.
- **A lent battery** earns research points like the rest, but no battery points: it is not yours to upgrade, and its research points go to your own research target. No battle lends one today (a Langer Max lent for the first battle was tried and taken out the same day; the basic training's borrowed Mörser 18 pair is a lesson and pays nothing).
- **Leaving early** keeps everything earned until then (only the victory bonus is lost).

**The battle summary.** When a battle ends, or you leave it with Main menu, a summary page shows how it ended, the research points and where they went (the battery being researched, before and after, and anything researched), each battery's rounds, kills and points, the counts of what was destroyed, knocked out and killed, and the biggest actions. If you close the page during a battle, the menu shows its summary the next time it opens.

## How fast batteries come

Worked out on 2026-11-26 with the game's own research rule on the default research targets, winning levels 1 to 4 once each and then level 4 again, at a human's pay for the new lengths (about 500 RP for a level 1 win, 600 at level 2, 850 at level 3 and 1,600 at level 4 with its secondary done; the all-auto crews' level 1 win pays 450 to 520) plus each level's first-win bonus:

| Battle (its RP with the bonus) | Researched, with the first-win bonus | Without it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 (level 1, +750 RP) | K 17 (rank II opens) | K 17 |
| 2 (level 2, +850) | K 16 | K 16 |
| 3 (level 3, +1,150) | Mörser 16 | Mörser 16 |
| 4 (level 4, +2,000) | Beta-Gerät | none |
| 5 to 10 (level 4, +1,600 each) | Bertha, Nebelwerfer 41, Nebelwerfer 42 and leFH 18, sFH 18, Hummel, K 18 | Beta-Gerät, Bertha, Nebelwerfer 41 and 42, leFH 18 and sFH 18, none, Hummel and K 18 |

Since the tree's cuts (2026-11-26; before them, with the C/92 to start, the three cheap rank I batteries made it about two a battle through level 3: 14 in 10 battles with the bonus, 12 without):

- **About one battery a battle**, on top of the two you start with: 11 in 10 battles with or without the bonus, more than half of the 18 to research.
- **The first session.** The K 17 after the first four-minute battle, and with it rank II; the bonus also pulls you up the levels.
- **Lean or rich.** At ×0.75 pay, 9 batteries in 10 battles; at ×1.25, 13.

## Missions for the heavy and the long guns

The open battles now have targets made for the guns that are usually idle:

- **Deep targets** stand beyond every gun's reach but the long guns'. 1917: two depots and a corps HQ 13 to 16 km out, for the 18-inch railway gun. WW2: a supply depot, an airstrip and a corps HQ 11 to 15 km west, for the K 18 and the Gustav.
- **Heavy targets** are groups of concrete shelters only heavy shells break: the 9.2-inch, 15-inch and railway gun in 1917; the Mörser 18, the Karl and the Gustav in WW2. Light guns cannot take them.

One of each comes at a time, by day, from the bombardment on, with a 25-minute window and 90 points. They only appear when a battery in the field can serve them, and sooner when such a battery has been idle for 90 seconds.

## The day fits your lineup

The open battle's timetable was written for the whole Group. With your lineup in the field (`src/sim/lineup.js`):

- **Reach.** A mission no battery of yours can serve (out of range, out of arc, or the wrong gun for it) goes to another site of the same kind one can, or is left out. Counter-battery calls, convoys and trains only come for what you can reach.
- **Cadence.** Missions wait while your hands are full: about one at a time per battery, three at least. A free hand gets the waiting mission that best suits one of your batteries.
- **Work.** A battery idle for 80 seconds is given a site that suits it: concrete and hostile batteries for the heavies, lighter targets for the field guns, the balloon for the 60-pounders. A big lineup gets more to do.
- **No waiting.** When everything is done and nothing is coming, the day runs on as a time lapse after 15 seconds: the clock moves quickly to the next order, attack or the end of the day. At a threat level it is quicker: a mission done gets a 3 second beat, then the day runs on ("Ahead of time") and the next thing comes about 5 seconds after it.

Battles without a lineup (the tests, the WW1 sandbox) run the fixed day.

## What there is to play

The menu has three choices (since 2026-11-02, to keep choosing simple):

- **Open battle:** the career's battle at a threat level. Every battle pays.
- **Special missions:** the Paris Gun, Gustav, Karl-Gerät, V-1 and V-2, each its weapon's real use. **The first win pays an RP bonus** (half the weapon's research cost, at least 500; the Paris Gun's and the V-1's from their cost before they left the tree, 2,000 and 1,500). Each offers a guided first run, its lesson.
- **Training:** the basic training.

The 12 campaign missions are out of the menu: the open battle and the special missions do their work. Their code and their old URLs stay. Batteries come only from research.

## The hangar and the preview

The hangar shows the batteries you have unlocked: the one you pick stands on parade with all the guns it has earned, each gun's crew in ranks in front of it. The crews are the guns' real minimal crews (10 for a 6-inch howitzer, 250 for the Gustav; [HISTORY.md](HISTORY.md#gun-detachments) has the figures and sources), plus any relief crews. On the Research screen, **Preview the full battery** opens any battery at full strength, every gun with its crew, without researching it.

## Dev mode

The **Dev mode** switch in the top right corner of the menu bar is off by default. Switched on, it puts every battery in the field in battle, researched and at its best. Your own career still earns underneath, and the hangar and the Research screen always show it. The choice is saved.

**Unlimited points**, the switch under it, makes research and modifications free for testing: the tree order still holds, nothing is spent, and the points earned keep adding up. It is off by default and saved.

**Unlimited points** also adds a "Research now" button to a battery that can be researched.

**Reset**, beside the switches, starts a new career (two clicks: the second within 4 seconds): no research, upgrades, points or battles, and the special missions and the threat levels unwon, so their RP bonuses can be won again. The switches stay as they are.

## Saves and tests

- **Save:** `drumfire.campaign.v4` in browser storage: the research target and the RP put into each battery, the batteries researched, each battery's points and levels, the special missions won (`specials`), the battles fought, the threat levels won (`wins`, since 2026-11-26: a save from before has every first win still to come), the switches.
