# Research, upgrades and the special missions

Germany has one career and one research tree: the guns of 1914 to 1918 and of the Second World War together, as a War Thunder nation. Britain (the 1917 game) is hidden from the menu for now; its tree is kept for when it comes back.

You start with one gun of the **15 cm Ringkanone C/92** and no upgrades, and fight the **open battle of 1918** on the ridge with the batteries you have researched. Every battle pays, won or lost. The pay goes into two purses:

- **Research points (RP)** go to the battery you are researching, picked on the Research screen. There is always one while anything is left; when it is done, the rest of the RP goes on to the next one in its line (or the cheapest open one).
- **Battery points**, per battery: each battery earns its own from its own fire, and spends them on its own upgrades, whenever you like.

## The tree

Seven lines, one type each, and each runs from the guns of 1914 to 1918 to those of the Second World War. A line is researched in order, and lines never cross. The lines that start at rank I (field artillery, long-range guns, rockets) are open from the start; the others open once you research a battery in the line beside them:

- **Mortars** after the 15 cm sFH 13 (field artillery).
- **Siege guns** after the 21 cm Mörser 16 (mortars).
- **Railway guns** after the 15 cm K 16 (long-range guns).
- **Off-map support** after the schweres Wurfgerät 40 (rockets).

The rank (I to IV) is the battery's weight class and scales its upgrade costs.

| Line | Batteries in order (rank, RP) |
| --- | --- |
| Field artillery | 7.7 cm FK 16 (I, 300) → 15 cm sFH 93 (I, 350) → 15 cm sFH 13 (I, 450) → 10.5 cm leFH 18 (I, 600) → 15 cm sFH 18 (II, 1,100) → Wespe (II, 1,500) → Hummel (II, 2,000) |
| Long-range guns | 15 cm Ringkanone C/92 (I, yours) → 10 cm K 17 (I, 350) → 15 cm K 16 (II, 700) → 17 cm K 18 (II, 1,100) → 21 cm K 38 (III, 2,000) |
| Railway guns | 38 cm Langer Max (III, 2,400) → 28 cm Kurze Bruno (III, 2,800) → 28 cm K5 (III, 3,400) → Paris Gun (IV, 4,000) → 21 cm K 12 (IV, 4,600) → Gustav (IV, 5,000) |
| Mortars | 21 cm Mörser 99 (II, 600) → 21 cm Mörser 16 (II, 900) → 21 cm Mörser 18 (II, 1,300) → 35.5 cm H M1 (III, 2,600) |
| Siege guns | 30.5 cm Beta-Gerät (III, 2,000) → 42 cm Gamma (III, 2,400) → 42 cm Bertha (III, 2,800) → Karl-Gerät (IV, 4,000) |
| Rockets | Nebelwerfer 41 (I, 450) → schweres Wurfgerät 40 (II, 800) → Nebelwerfer 42 (II, 1,200) |
| Off-map support | Gotha strike (III, 2,000) → V-1 (IV, 3,000) → V-2 (IV, 4,500) |

What the new batteries bring (2026-11-01):

- **FK 16.** The quickest gun in the tree: shrapnel over infantry, the Sperrfeuer and the Feuerwalze. Its light shell does nothing to tanks or concrete.
- **The ramp guns: Ringkanone C/92, sFH 93, Mörser 99.** No recoil system: at every shot the wheels run up two ramps and the gun rolls back down by its own weight, never quite on its old line, so the crew levers it round with handspikes and lays it afresh. Every round carries a new laying error, so each falls like a ranging round. Better sights shrink it.
- **Your first battery: the Ringkanone C/92** (since 2026-11-01; the K 17 before). An old fortress gun, slow and a little wild, but its 40 kg shell hits harder than the K 17's 18 kg. The K 17 is the first thing to research down its line. A career saved before the change keeps its K 17 and gets the C/92 as well.
- **Schweres Wurfgerät.** Short reach (two kilometres), dug in behind our front trench. With it in the lineup the British forward posts (Lewis guns and a Stokes mortar) appear opposite as targets.
- **Taken out (2026-11-02): the 25 cm schwerer Minenwerfer.** A kilometre's reach from behind our front trench left it two targets on the whole ridge. A career that researched it gets its 300 RP back.
- **Beta-Gerät.** The Gamma's flow on a smaller bed: T relays the bed past its arc.
- **K 16, K 38.** Long guns: counter-battery and the depots behind the British line.
- **H M1.** A 35.5 cm siege howitzer on its platform, high angle only.
- **Kurze Bruno, K 12.** Railway guns laid for line by shunting along their curved spurs: a slow traverse. The K 12 stands far back and reaches everything on the map.
- **K5.** On a Vögele turntable: any bearing, at about 0.4° a second, so a target across the map costs minutes.
- **Wespe, Hummel.** Self-propelled: T drives the battery to its second position. The enemy loses it on the way, but the guns are out of action for a couple of minutes and the new position needs ranging. An auto crew moves by itself when it is found.
- **Gotha strike.** Three bombers cross the map from the east at 1,800 m and each drops a stick of four 100 kg bombs across the point you gave.

- **Off-map support.** The Gotha strike, the V-1, the V-2 and the Paris Gun (whose targets are 120 km away) fight in the open battle from off the map: you pick one, give it a target, and it fires in its own time, then takes long to be ready again. Their own launch missions stay, open to all.
- **The other battles.** The special missions are open to all, as before. The WW2 open battle is out of the menu for now: the higher threat levels are to bring the career onto its map.
- **Old saves.** A career keeps every battery it has researched. The Langer Max, the Paris Gun and the Gustav moved to the railway line and the Gamma, Bertha and the Karl to the siege line, so a career part way down the old long-range or mortar line now has the batteries before them still to research (owning a later battery never blocks you).
- **Growing the tree further.** Ideas per line: the 24 cm K 3; the leFH 16 and a leFH 18/40 folder; the 21 cm Mörser 10; the Wurfrahmen 40; Stuka strikes off the map (as the Gotha's WW2 counterpart).

## Your lineup

As in War Thunder's crew slots, at most **5 batteries on the map and 1 off it** take the field. Choose them in the hangar: its bar along the bottom holds only the lineup's slots, each with a swap (⇄) that opens the bench (the batteries you have outside the lineup, on the map or off it as the slot is), where picking one puts it in that slot or the slot is left empty; an empty slot opens the bench at once, and "Leave out of battle" beside the battery empties its slot. Until you choose, the lineup is the best battery of each line (the first five lines', as there are five slots), then more field artillery (the Sperrfeuer's guns), then the heaviest; off the map the dearest. Dev mode opens every battery to the lineup, at its best, but the slots stay the same.

## Threat levels

The career's open battle is played at a **threat level** from 1 to 10 (as Helldivers 2's difficulties), picked in the battle chooser beside To battle. Level 1 is a quiet morning a lone Ringkanone C/92 can carry; level 10 asks everything of a full lineup at its best. A level sets the battle, never your own guns: its orders, the attacks and their size, the counter-battery fire, the aircraft, the kinds of target, the deadlines, and its pay (×1 up to ×4).

- **It ends on its orders, never on the clock:** finish the level's fire missions (1 at level 1, up to 10 at level 10), then hold through any attacks still to come; levels 1 to 4 are then won, and from level 5 our own attack at X-Zeit follows and decides it. Too many failed missions lose (3 allowed at level 1, 6 from level 5; convoys and trains that get away do not count). The box at the top of the 3D view shows the count.
- **All open:** every level can be played from the start, in any order.
- **Strongpoints (levels 6 to 10):** concrete strongpoints in the lanes of our X-Zeit assault, two at levels 6 and 7, three at 8 and 9, four at 10. Only shells of 28 cm and more break them: the Langer Max, the Kurze Bruno, the K5, the H M1, the Beta-Gerät, the Gamma, Bertha and the Karl (Bertha reaches only the two in the southern lane from its pit). Lighter shells do nothing to them. Each one still standing at X-Zeit fires on our men in its lane, whatever the Feuerwalze does. Their missions open after the registration, by day and by night, with no clock: one at a time for each battery that can take them. So from level 6 one of the five slots wants a super-heavy, which costs a field battery's Sperrfeuer; the share of men the assault needs is a little lower there to make up for it.
- **Modifiers:** from level 5 one, from level 8 two, rolled for each battle and shown before it: ground mist, enemy air superiority, a shell shortage, enemy sound ranging, poor intelligence, rain.
- **The card** for each level says what its day holds and the lineup it fits.
- The other battles (the WW2 sandbox, the special weapons' operations, the campaign) keep Easy, Normal and Hard.

## Upgrades

**Every battery starts with one gun.** Its other guns come one at a time, into its empty gun pits. Each battery also has seven parts with three levels each:

- **Shells**: +12%, +25%, +40% damage. In the hangar the shells beside each gun show it: a fresh coat and a green band, then a white band and a taller fuze, then a pointed ballistic cap.
- **Barrel**: 15%, 28%, 40% tighter fall of shot, and less barrel wear.
- **Breech and loading gear**: 10%, 19%, 28% faster loading.
- **Sights and survey**: the first round lands 15%, 30%, 45% closer, and the guns lay faster.
- **Camouflage**: 12%, 24%, 36% less flash and noise for the enemy to locate.
- **Ammunition supply**: +20%, +40%, +60% shells in hand and faster resupply.
- **Relief crews**: +25%, +50%, +75% men at each gun, to take turns at the heavy work so the crew stays fresh (fatigue itself is not modelled). In the game the extra men are reserves, so a gun slows only when casualties take it below its drill's number, and the fresher hands load 3%, 6%, 10% faster. They stand in the hangar's ranks.

You buy them in any order with the battery's own points. Costs rise with the level and the battery's tier: a tier I part costs 80, 170 and 300, and the second gun 120.

## What a battle pays

Rewards come **as you earn them**, as in War Thunder and Enlisted: each one shows in the reward feed on the right of the 3D view (what it was, the battery that did it, the research points), with the battle's running total above it, and goes into your career at once.

- **Kills.** A target's score is paid half as its parts are knocked out (a gun of a battery, a tank of a laager, a lorry of a convoy, a stack of a dump) and half when it is destroyed.
- **Enemy casualties:** half a point for each of the enemy's men a burst kills.
- **Hits:** a quarter point for every point of damage, gathered into one line per target.
- **Missions:** a mission done shares its points among the batteries that fired on it, by rounds.
- **RP** are the batteries' total, plus 4 for every minute in action and 400 for a battle won, paid at the end. Each battery keeps its own share as battery points.
- **The threat level** scales it all (×1 at level 1 to ×4 at level 10); the other battles' difficulty does (Easy 0.8, Normal 1, Hard 1.35).
- **Leaving early** keeps everything earned until then (only the victory bonus is lost).

**The battle summary.** When a battle ends, or you leave it with Main menu, a summary page shows how it ended, the research points and where they went (the battery being researched, before and after, and anything researched), each battery's rounds, kills and points, the counts of what was destroyed, knocked out and killed, and the biggest actions. If you close the page during a battle, the menu shows its summary the next time it opens.

## Missions for the heavy and the long guns

The open battles now have targets made for the guns that are usually idle:

- **Deep targets** stand beyond every gun's reach but the long guns'. 1917: two depots and a corps HQ 13 to 16 km out, for the 18-inch railway gun. WW2: a supply depot, an airstrip and a corps HQ 11 to 15 km west, for the K 18 and the Gustav.
- **Heavy targets** are groups of concrete shelters only heavy shells break: the 9.2-inch, 15-inch and railway gun in 1917; the Mörser 18, the Karl and the Gustav in WW2. Light guns cannot take them.

One of each comes at a time, by day, from the bombardment on, with a 25-minute window and 90 points. They only appear when a battery in the field can serve them, and sooner when such a battery has been idle for 90 seconds.

## The day fits your lineup

The open battle's timetable was written for the whole Group. With your lineup in the field (`src/sim/lineup.js`):

- **Reach.** A mission no battery of yours can serve (out of range, out of arc, or the wrong gun for it) goes to another site of the same kind one can, or is left out. Counter-battery calls, convoys and trains only come for what you can reach.
- **Cadence.** Missions wait while your hands are full: about one at a time per battery, three at least. A free hand gets the waiting mission that best suits one of your batteries.
- **Work.** A battery idle for 80 seconds is given a site that suits it: concrete and hostile batteries for the heavies, lighter targets for the field guns, the balloon for the 60-pounders. A big lineup gets more to do.
- **No waiting.** When everything is done and nothing is coming, the day runs on as a time lapse after 15 seconds: the clock moves quickly to the next order, attack or the end of the day.

Battles without a lineup (the tests, the WW1 sandbox) run the fixed day.

## What there is to play

The menu has three choices (since 2026-11-02, to keep choosing simple):

- **Open battle:** the career's battle at a threat level. Every battle pays.
- **Special missions:** the Paris Gun, Gustav, Karl-Gerät, V-1 and V-2, each its weapon's real use. **The first win pays an RP bonus** (half the weapon's research cost, at least 500). Each offers a guided first run, its lesson.
- **Training:** the basic training.

The 12 campaign missions are out of the menu: the open battle and the special missions do their work. Their code and their old URLs stay. Batteries come only from research.

Every part shows in the hangar, level by level. Hover it in the modifications panel and the camera goes there; before the first level is bought, to the place where it will stand. A battery short of guns has the next gun's place pegged out and taped at the end of its line, and Guns' hover goes there.

The camouflage paint is on the battle's guns too, and the radio names a relief taking a fallen man's place. The muzzle cover and the open breech are for the parade only: a gun in action has neither.

## The hangar and the preview

The hangar shows the batteries you have unlocked: the one you pick stands on parade with all the guns it has earned, each gun's crew in ranks in front of it. The crews are the guns' real minimal crews (10 for a 6-inch howitzer, 250 for the Gustav; [HISTORY.md](HISTORY.md#gun-detachments) has the figures and sources), plus any relief crews. On the Research screen, **Preview the full battery** opens any battery at full strength, every gun with its crew, without researching it.

## Dev mode

The **Dev mode** switch in the top right corner of the menu bar is off by default. Switched on, it puts every battery in the field in battle, researched and at its best. Your own career still earns underneath, and the hangar and the Research screen always show it. The choice is saved.

**Unlimited points**, the switch under it, makes research and modifications free for testing: the tree order still holds, nothing is spent, and the points earned keep adding up. It is off by default and saved.

**Unlimited points** also adds a "Research now" button to a battery that can be researched.

**Reset**, beside the switches, starts a new career (two clicks: the second within 4 seconds): no research, upgrades, points or battles, and the special missions unwon, so their RP bonus can be won again. The switches stay as they are.

## Saves and tests

- **Save:** `drumfire.campaign.v4` in browser storage: the research target and the RP put into each battery, the batteries researched, each battery's points and levels, the special missions won (`specials`), the battles fought, the switches. Saves from before the German tree are not carried over: a new career.
- **Code:** the trees, parts, costs and pay are in `src/research.js`, the career in `src/progression.js`, the screens in `src/ui/menu.js` (the hangar's panels, `researchView`) with the 3D hangar in `src/ui/hangar.js`, the heavy and long guns' missions in `src/sim/specialists.js`. DESIGN.md has the contract.
- **Tests:** `node tools/progressiontest.mjs` checks research, upgrades, pay, saves and the campaign missions. `node tools/researchtest.mjs [seeds] --era de --lineup start|early|mid|full|max|all` plays headless open battles with a career's lineups and reports wins, RP and battery points.
