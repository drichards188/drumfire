# How to play

Back to the [README](../README.md).

This page covers the menu, research and the campaigns, every era's units and operations, the special weapons' drills, the Big Bertha demo, every key and the tutorials.

## The menu

- **The top bar.** The nation (Germany, with its 1918 and WW2 entries; Britain is hidden for now), then a screen: **Hangar** or **Research** (with how far the battery being researched has got beside it).
- **Loading.** The menu opens behind a loading screen that lifts once the hangar is fully drawn.
- **Hangar.** Your unlocked batteries. The one you pick is drawn up on a parade ground for inspection: every gun it has earned side by side, each gun's real minimal crew in ranks in front of it (in blocks for the biggest guns), plus any relief crews, an inspecting officer and his sergeant facing them. The railway guns stand on their track, their crews either side of it. Drag to orbit, the wheel to zoom; the camera turns slowly by itself. The battery's figures are on the left, its **modifications** (guns and parts to buy) on the right, and your lineup's slots along the bottom (5 on the map, 1 off it): click one, or Left and Right; a slot's swap button (⇄) opens the batteries you have that are not in it, and picking one puts it in that slot. With dev mode on, the parade shows every battery at its best, as it would take the field. Beside each gun stand its shells and its powder (bagged charges or brass cases), the shells marked by the battery's Shells upgrade: a fresh coat and a green band (I), a white band and a taller fuze (II), a pointed ballistic cap (III).
- **Upgrades you can see.** Every modification shows on parade: Barrel (sponge staves and a bucket on a groundsheet, then the star gauge on its trestles, then a canvas cover on the muzzle), Breech (the rammer and shell tongs, then oil cans and the artificer's chest, then the breech opened and oiled with a round on the loading tray), Sights (red-and-white aiming posts, then the director on its tripod, then the plotting table under an awning), Camouflage (disruptive paint, then hessian screens along the gun's right and back, then the net drawn back off the gun and hung behind it), Ammunition supply (more of the gun's own shells piled behind its display, one pyramid, then two, then three, and the powder heaped higher with them), Relief crews (a squad of their own in working dress). Hover a modification and the camera goes to it, with a ring on the ground under it; before you buy the first level, to the place where it will stand. The next gun's place is pegged out at the end of the line.
- **In battle** the guns wear the same camouflage paint, and the radio says when a relief takes a fallen man's place.
- **Research.** The German tree in its five lines: click a battery to see it, make it the battery to research, or upgrade it. "Preview the full battery" opens it in the hangar at full strength, every gun with its crew, with a way back to Research.
- **Test range.** "Try on the range" (in the hangar under a battery's figures, and on Research) opens the test range with one gun of that battery; the Battles list no longer has a Test range entry. See [The test range](#the-test-range).
- **To battle.** The dropdown beside the To battle button chooses what to play: the Battles, Campaign and Tutorials lists, the chosen one's orders, and the difficulty (Easy, Normal or Hard). To battle, Enter, or a double-click on an entry plays it; in the dropdown Up and Down walk the list and Esc closes it. The battlefield loads only then, behind a loading screen with the mission's orders. The "Main menu" button in the game goes back, and the end card's Research button goes straight to the Research screen.
- **Memory and sound.** The menu remembers its choices in the browser. Sound starts on the first click or key press.
- **The screen.** In the game, the left half of the screen is a third-person view of the unit you have selected; the right half is its fire control console.
- **AI crews.** Units you are not operating fight on their own with an AI crew, in whatever mode you left them.

## Research, upgrades and the campaign

- **Start small.** Germany is one career: you start with the **10 cm Kanone 17** with **one gun** and no upgrades. The open battle of 1918 (the ridge) fields the batteries you have researched, 1918 and WW2 guns together, with the guns and parts they have earned.
- **Every battle pays**, won or lost: research points (RP) to the battery you are researching, and battery points to each battery for its own fire. Leaving early pays what was earned so far.
- **Research.** The Research screen (top bar) shows the tree in five lines (long-range guns, field howitzers, mortars and siege, rockets, off-map support). A line is researched in order; the first of every line is open from the start. Click a battery, then "Research this battery": every battle's RP go to it until it is done, and the rest goes on to the next one in its line. See docs/PROGRESSION.md for the lines and costs.
- **Your lineup.** At most 5 batteries on the map and 1 off-map support take the field: swap them in the hangar's slot bar, or "Leave out of battle" beside the battery. Dev mode opens every battery to the lineup but keeps the 5 slots.
- **Threat levels.** The career's open battle is played at a level from 1 to 10, picked in the battle chooser; each played to its end, won or lost, opens the next. See docs/PROGRESSION.md.
- **Off-map support.** The V-1, the V-2 and the Paris Gun fight in the open battle from off the map. Their tiles sit under the battery tabs, marked OFF-MAP SUPPORT. Click a ready tile, then click the board where it is to fall (a point, or a target's symbol). It prepares (PREP), flies (AIR) and strikes in its own time, then reloads for a long time (RLD; about 9 to 13 minutes, less with its breech and relief crews upgraded).
  - **Changing or stopping it.** Click a preparing tile, then the board, to give it a new point; its × stands it down with no reload.
  - **On the board.** Each weapon's aim shows as a ring two spreads across with its countdown, and the last strike stays marked for a minute and a half.
  - **The arrival.** The V-1 comes in low and buzzing and dives in silence once its motor cuts. The V-2 is not heard until it strikes. The Paris shell falls steeply. F rides it in while it is over the map (when the selected battery has no shell of its own in the air).
  - **When it can't fire.** A tile that can't take an order (in the air, reloading) says why on the radio.
- **Upgrades.** In the hangar's modifications panel (or on the Research screen), a researched battery spends its own points on more guns (up to its full battery) and on seven parts with three levels each: shells (damage), barrel (accuracy), breech and loading gear (reload), sights and survey (first round and laying), camouflage (less for the enemy to locate), ammunition supply, and relief crews (more men at each gun to take turns at the heavy work: reserves against casualties, and a little faster loading). Any order.
- **The enemy follows your strength.** A small lineup meets less counter-battery fire and smaller attacks.
- **Heavy and long guns' missions.** "Deep target" missions stand beyond every gun's reach but the long guns' (the 18-inch railway gun; the K 18 and the Gustav). "Heavy target" missions are concrete shelters only heavy shells break (the 9.2-inch and up; the Mörser 18, the Karl and the Gustav). They come by day, one of each at a time, only when you field a battery that can take them.
- **Campaign missions** are one-off challenges in any order: each loans its battery, with every earlier battery of its era beside it on auto, each with its own target under counter-battery fire. Finish every assignment with the right fuze and enough rounds before the time runs out (a V-1 or V-2 launch counts within 1 degree and 1 % of range, with no oxygen shortfall). The first win pays an RP bonus; it does not grant the battery.
- **Always open.** The WW2 Gustav, Karl-Gerät, V-1 and V-2 operations and every lesson are open from the start, with their whole roster.
- **Saving.** Progress is saved in the browser.
- **WW1 German** has no career: every battery is yours from the start.
- **Dev mode.** A switch in the top right corner of the menu bar puts every battery in the open battles, researched and fully upgraded, for testing. It is on by default for now; your own career still earns underneath and the hangar and the Research screen show it. Switch it off to play it.
- **Unlimited points.** The switch under it makes research and modifications cost nothing (the tree order still holds), for testing. Off by default; the points you earn keep adding up underneath.
- **Reset.** The red button beside the switches starts a new career: research, upgrades, points, battles and campaign wins back to nothing. Click it, then click "Confirm?" within 4 seconds. The switches stay as they are.

Details, costs and numbers: [PROGRESSION.md](PROGRESSION.md).

## The test range

A quiet range on the era's own battle map, in daylight, with one gun of the battery you chose (earned or not) and nothing else: no other battery is drawn or in play. Nothing is scored or paid.

- **The card** on the left of the 3D view has three tabs. **Targets:** every target on the map, nearest first, with its range and whether the gun reaches it; **Mission** gives it an observed mission (the aeroplane reports each fall), **Lay** lays it on the target, and the board works as in battle. **Upgrades:** the seven parts at any level; a change starts the range again with them (their effect, not their look). **Debug:** an invulnerable gun and endless ammunition (both on), no errors (no wind, calibration, map error or dispersion), and buttons for hostile fire on the gun, an attack with its SOS, a convoy, a train, clearing the range and copying a link to the setup.
- **In person.** On the WW2 guns that can be served in person, P (or Man the gun) puts you at the gun and the drill fires it, as in battle; the card steps aside while you are there.
- **Keys** are the battle's: Space fires, `[` and `]` change the time, H hides the panels. Another gun: go back to the menu and use Try on the range on its card.

The 1917 battle is "Ypres salient" in the menu's Battles tab. The Group has five units (in the battle, the ones you have researched):

| Key | Unit | Call sign | Battery | Guns |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 60-pounders | Long Tom | 1/1st Lowland Heavy Battery | 2 |
| 2 | 6-inch 26 cwt howitzers | Ginger | 118th Siege Battery | 4 |
| 3 | 9.2-inch howitzer | Mother | 91st Siege Battery | 2 |
| 4 | 15-inch howitzer | Granny | No. 3 RMA Howitzer | 1 |
| 5 | 18-inch railway howitzer | Wolfe | No. 13 Railway Siege Battery | 1 |

- **The heavies are slow and powerful.** The 15-inch loads in about 50 s, the 18-inch railway gun in about 90 s, the 60-pounder in about 10 s.
- **Each kind has its own loading crew and machinery:** the 15-inch shell crane, the railway gun's shell hoist and tray, the pair carrying a 9.2-inch round from the stack.
- **Shrapnel.** The 60-pounders can also fire shrapnel (airbursts over wire and open ground).
- **The 18-inch railway gun** (2,500 lb shells, about 20 km) reaches the deep targets (railhead, headquarters, far batteries) and fires the biggest burst of the 1917 battle. It is a what-if gun (see [History and sources](HISTORY.md)).

### How a fire mission works

1. **A mission arrives.** The message pad and the mission list show it: a hostile battery (counter-battery), a pillbox or other point target to destroy, wire to cut, harassing fire on a road or the railhead, or an SOS. Missions carry a priority and a deadline; an SOS has none, it lasts as long as the attack. The observer is one of these:
   - a forward observation officer (FOO) by telephone;
   - an aeroplane by wireless (by day);
   - sound rangers, who give a map reference with some error (hostile batteries at night);
   - rail spotters on the railhead;
   - or nobody: a map shoot.
2. **Lay the guns.** Take the mission (Tab, Enter) or click the board. The console computes the line (degrees and minutes of arc), the range (yards), the charge and the elevation from the range table, which comes from the same integrator the simulation flies shells with. The order card says when a target is out of range, inside the minimum range or outside the gun's arc of fire. Each piece traverses and elevates to the order; the loading crew works through the loading steps.
3. **Ranging.** The first round of a fresh lay lands 50 to 400 m off (on Normal; less on Easy, more on Hard). Fire one round and wait for the report.
   - **A FOO** telephones the fall of shot in yards (add or drop, left or right of the line of fire).
   - **Aircraft** send the RFC clock code: a circle letter for the distance (Y 10, Z 25, A 50, B 100, C 200, D 300, E 400, F 500 yards) and an hour for the direction, with 12 o'clock true north from the target.
   - **Correct** with the arrows or `.` and fire again; good corrections get within about 30 m in two or three rounds.
   - **Errors:** wind (the day's meteor telegram), gun wear and each gun's calibration error all shift the fall of shot.
4. **Fire for effect.** Once on, raise the rounds (R) and fire. All pieces of the unit shoot a series. The target's parts (each gun of a battery, each stack of a dump, each wagon) are damaged by a table of calibre against hardness, and a pillbox needs heavier shells or more of them. Dumps cook off in chains.
   - **No report on a series:** to check the fall or correct, set ROUNDS back to 1 and fire a ranging round. It is answered even when another battery on the same task is already firing for effect.
5. **Counter-battery.** Every round builds the unit's signature (flash and sound ranging), more at night and, by day, while the German kite balloon is up. German batteries walk fire onto the loudest unit; the incoming scream warns you about four seconds ahead. Go to HOLD to let the signature fade, or silence the hostile battery itself.
6. **SOS.** When the Germans form up for an assault, the hurricane bombardment is a warning, then red rockets go up over a sector (A Frezenberg, B Zonnebeke road, C Polygon, D Gheluvelt). Press S, or click the sector's SOS button: every unit that can reach the line lays on it and fires. Infantry that reach the British trench breach the sector; two lost sectors lose the game.
7. **Zero hour.** In the last phase the British infantry go forward behind a creeping barrage that lifts on a timetable. Keep the guns on the lane and lift on time. The game is won when enough of the infantry reach the objective (50 % on Easy, 60 % on Normal, 70 % on Hard). It is lost when two front sectors fall or every gun is destroyed.

### The day and the map

- **The day** runs as a time lapse of about 40 minutes of game time in five phases: Registration (a quiet dawn that teaches the loop), the bombardment, the counter-attack, Night (harassing fire, star shells, the railhead) and Zero hour.
- **Map references** use the British 1:20,000 trench map system for sheet 28: a lettered 6000-yard square, a numbered 1000-yard square, a quarter a to d and tenths of 50 yards, for example `J.14.c.3.7`.

Pick one in the menu's Battles tab, or open it directly with `&play=1`. The four special-weapon operations are open from the start, each with its whole roster.

### Serving a gun in person

Press **P**, or **MAN** on a gun's row of the guns card, to take a place in the detachment of a gun of the selected battery: any German gun of the open battles, the 1918 ones on the ridge (K 17, sFH 13, 21 cm Mörser 16, Langer Max, Gamma, Bertha) as well as the WW2 ones (leFH 18, sFH 18, K 18, 21 cm Mörser 18, Nebelwerfer, Gustav, Karl-Gerät). Not the V-1, the V-2 or the Paris Gun: their own missions have drills of their own. The view goes to first person at that man's eyes; the console beside it still commands the whole Gruppe. P (or LEAVE) takes you back; the crew carries on where you left it.

- **The posts.** 1 Geschützführer (reads back each new fire command, hold E), 2 Richtkanonier (A and D traverse, W and S elevate, Shift fine), 3 Ladekanonier (open, shell, ram, cartridge, close: hold E), 4 Abzugskanonier (fit the primer, then the pull: hold E). The heavies' barrel first runs down to its loading angle (S at post 2).
- **The gun card.** At the top, the one thing to do now and its key (or the post to go to, or that the crew is on it), with the hold's progress. Below it, the fire command for your gun beside the gun's own lay (Seite and Erhöhung in Strich, what is left to lay and the key that lays it, the charge, the fuze), whether it is read back and whether Feuer is ordered, and the drill step by step in the colours of the posts that work them. The keys listed first are those of your post.
- **The wheels** slow over the last few Strich and stop on the commanded figure; let go and press again to go past it.
- **The Nebelwerfer** has its own drill: the loader loads the six rockets and connects the igniters, and the firer takes the crew into cover (hold E at post 4) and fires the ripple from the firing box (hold E); you watch the launcher from the slit trench.
- **The Gustav, the Karl-Gerät, the Gamma and the Langer Max** lay by power, slowly (the Gustav takes about two minutes from 40 degrees to its loading angle). Tap A, D, W or S at post 2 to start the drive: it runs on by itself and stops on the commanded figure, even if you go to another post meanwhile; tap the other way to stop it, hold Shift for the hand wheel. The card counts the drive down; **]** speeds time. Their long steps (the Gustav's shell hoist, the Karl's crane) take at most 8 s of holding E when you work them yourself. The Karl's turn of the whole vehicle stays on the KARL card, which keeps its keys at the gun: L raises or lowers the hull, T turns, Y sends the carrier. The Gustav at the siege can be manned once it stands on its curve.
- **Crew help (C).** On, the men at the other posts do their work; off, nothing at another post moves until you go there, as in the Big Bertha demo. Remembered between games; also in the gear menu.
- **Fire orders.** On AUTO the battery's own commander takes the missions and orders Feuer; your gun fires when you pull. An auto battery does not wait for ever: a fire order your gun has not moved on for 40 s lapses. On MANUAL you give the orders from the console, and a pull with no fire order fires your gun alone, the rounds per gun set on the console (R).
- **The other batteries** on manual go to auto while you are at a gun and back to manual when you leave (gear menu: "At a gun: other batteries to auto").

The Nebelwerfer (fired from cover) and the V-1 and V-2 launch sites (drills of their own) cannot be served this way.

### Crew voices

In the WW2 open battle (Flanders), the Karl-Gerät operation and the WW1 German open battle the selected battery's crew and the telephone speak German, with the words and an English translation as subtitles over the 3D view. **V**, or the VOICES button at the foot of the console's battery tabs, switches them on and off (remembered between games); M mutes them with everything else.

- **At the guns,** heard from where they stand, after the sound's travel time: the Batterieoffizier gives the Feuerkommando for a new target ("Achtung! Neues Ziel! Granate, Aufschlagzünder ohne Verzögerung! Ladung fünf!"), the corrections ("Verbesserung! Neu richten!"), the fire orders ("Erstes Geschütz, Feuer!", "Wirkungsschießen! Vier Schuss! Batterie, Feuer!", "Werfer, Feuer!", "Sperrfeuer, Feuer frei!"), "Batterie, halt! Feuer einstellen!", "Batterie feuerbereit!" and the alarms ("Volle Deckung!", "Fliegeralarm!", "Tiefflieger!", "Sanitäter!"). The detachment of No. 1 gun (or the gun you serve) calls each step of its loading cycle ("Verschluss auf!", "Granate kommt!", "Granate ansetzen!", "Kartusche rein!", "Verschluss zu!") and reports "Erstes Geschütz feuerbereit!". The Nebelwerfer crew loads its tubes and goes into cover; the Karl-Gerät's crew drives, swings the vehicle, lowers the hull and calls the ammunition carrier.
- **On the telephone and the radio:** the Abteilung gives new fire missions ("Hier Abteilung, neuer Feuerauftrag, feindliche Batterie erkannt, Gegenbatteriefeuer."), the red flares of a Sperrfeuer, the Feuerwalze's lifts and the end of a mission; the battery reports each volley to the observer ("Hier Batterie, abgefeuert, Achtung auf den Einschlag."); the VB by telephone, or the air observer by radio, reports the fall of shot with the same figures as the message pad ("Hier Beobachtung. Zu kurz, weiter 150. Links 50.", "Ziel erfasst, Schuss im Ziel, Wirkungsschießen!", "Wirkung im Ziel"). These calls also appear in the radio log.
- Only the selected battery's crew speaks; select another battery and its crew takes over. At high time compression some lines are left out rather than spoken late.
- The voices are synthetic (generated offline with Piper; see [public/audio/CREDITS.md](../public/audio/CREDITS.md) and [VOICES.md](VOICES.md)). The other WW2 operations, the Paris Gun's mission and the 1917 game have no voices yet. In 1918 the crews use the same recordings; the air observer's reports come by telephone (he sent them by wireless telegraphy, not by voice).

## WW1 German: the Gruppe in 1918

In the menu, **WW1 German** switches to a third mode (`/?mode=ww1&play=1`): the heavy artillery of a German Gruppe on the ridge east of Ypres in the spring of 1918, firing west at the British side of the same map.

- **The career's open battle.** "Open battle" fields the batteries your career has researched (dev mode: every one), the WW2 ones too; the Paris Gun has its own mission ("Paris Gun", `/?mode=ww1&op=paris&play=1`), and in the open battle it is off-map support.
- **The targets** include the British game's own guns, standing in their own pits (the 15-inch, the 9.2-inch pair, the 6-inch battery, the 60-pounders and the 18-inch railway gun), with 18-pounder batteries, Mark IV tanks, dumps, road traffic and the railhead.
- **The enemy in the air.** RE8 spotters circle over whichever unit has been firing most and make the British counter-battery accurate; Sopwith Camels come in low with bombs.
- **The console** speaks metres and Strich, German grid references ("7037 b 13": a 1 km square, its quarter, a 100 m square) and German words.

The battle's roster, smallest calibre first (keys 1 to 6):

1. 10 cm K 17 (two long guns, shrapnel too)
2. 15 cm sFH 13 (four heavy field howitzers)
3. 21 cm Mörser 16 (two heavy howitzers)
4. the 38 cm "Langer Max" (a battleship gun on its railway mounting, set down on a prepared bed)
5. the 42 cm M-Gerät "Dicke Bertha"
6. the 42 cm Gamma-Mörser (fixed on its bed, set up and ready)

- **The beds.** Bertha and the Gamma turn only 11.5 and 23 degrees either way on their beds. For a target beyond, press T (or BETTUNG UMLEGEN on the guns card) to relay the bed onto its line.
- **The day** runs like the WW2 Flanders battle: Einschießen at dawn, the Artilleriekampf, the British attack (Sperrfeuer, S), night and Störungsfeuer.
- **X-Zeit.** A three-minute Feuervorbereitung and the Feuerwalze for our own assault, which needs the barrage kept dense on every lane.

### The Paris Gun's mission (work in progress)

The mission plays like the WW2 Gustav and V-weapon missions, in two stages. First the **Stellungsbau**: the gun arrives by rail at an empty site, and the three work gangs you send to the tasks build the position. Then the **Feuerablauf**, round by round: gauge the bore and raise the charge with its wear, lay the firing data for the Earth's turning and the wind aloft, load the numbered shell, fire, and correct from the agents' fall reports.

**Building the position (STELLUNGSBAU)**

- **Watch it go up.** The STELLUNGSBAU card, in the guns card's place, lists the tasks: the concrete bed with the turntable and racer, the earth bank, the firing frame, the barrel lowered in by the portal crane, the truss that holds it straight, the field railway and the ammunition train, the weather station and the Rechenstelle, and (optional) the camouflage nets and flak posts.
- **Send the gangs.** Put the three work gangs on the tasks with + and − (a task waits for the ones it needs), or press J for all hands on the critical path.

**Firing (FEUERABLAUF)**

- **A round.** Once the gun stands, the card becomes the FEUERABLAUF, and Enter starts a round. The crew gauges the bore (the charge must rise with the wear: Q and E). The Rechenstelle works out the firing data (T lays the table for the Earth's turning and the day's wind aloft, with what the reports have taught). Then the crew loads the numbered shell and raises the barrel.
- **SCHUSSWERTE.** This firing data card, in the fire order card's place, holds the charge against the gauge and the line and range corrections against the table.
- **Aiming.** The gun lays itself on the target you pick, and you aim by correcting that lay: SEITE moves it left or right, ENTFERNUNG shorter or further.
- **Reports.** When the agents' report comes in, the card says where the round fell and what to correct, and REPORT (`.`) lays it for you. The blue ring on the map's close-up shows where the next round should fall and moves as you correct.
- **The map** shows the gun, the four targets far to the west with the orders on them, the band the gun can reach and where every round fell, with a close-up of the target laid on. Click a target to lay on it.
- **Help on hover.** Every control and label explains itself in English. The big key always says what the drill wants, and FEUER is yours (Enter or Space).
- **Faults.** Now and then a fault (a cut obturator, a slack truss) is fixed (O) or carried on through (I) at a risk to the round.

**Targets, wear and raids**

- **The targets.** The gun fires at four military targets about 120 km away, off the map: a railway junction, a tank depot, a reserve camp and an aerodrome. The Armee orders rounds on one or another (five orders; carry out four to win), listed on the ZIELE card in the range table's place (click one, or Tab and L, to lay on it).
- **The flight.** The camera rides each shell up out of sight by itself (F leaves the ride). The agents' report comes about two minutes later (`.` applies it).
- **Wear.** After 65 rounds the barrel is changed (Y); the mission opens on a barrel already well worn.
- **Air raids.** British Camels come looking for the site by day. While they attack, the crew is in cover; the camouflage and rounds on their aerodrome keep some of them away.
- **Auto.** The gun starts on manual; A hands it to the crew.

## Big Bertha (demo, work in progress)

A separate first-person demo (`/bertha.html`, in the Battles tab of the WW1 German era).

- **The gun:** one German 42 cm M-Gerät behind the Passchendaele ridge in 1917, and its detachment.
- **Your part:** take each man's place in turn: gun commander, layer for line, layer for elevation, loader, firer.
- **The drill:** take the fire order on the field telephone, lay the line through the panoramic sight, set the clinometer and crank the barrel, hoist, ram and charge the 810 kg shell, and pull the lanyard.
- **The enemy:** the British gun line 10 to 13 km to the west answers.
- **The title screen** has the difficulty, a crew-assist option (the stations you are not at work themselves), a tutorial (one round, key by key, in about five minutes) and an English or German switch. The menu opens the demo straight into a game; the **Title screen** button beside the gear leads there, and its link back to the main menu.

## Tutorials

- **Where.** The menu's Tutorials tab holds short guided lessons on a quiet scripted range, always in daylight: the sun is held at 10:00 and the weather is Clear or Overcast; your own settings come back when you leave.
- **How.** Each lesson shows an instruction card over the 3D view, pulses the console controls it is about, and completes a step when you have done what it asks. Skip step and Esc are always there.
- **Always open.** Lessons are open whatever you have researched, and completed ones are ticked (stored in the browser).

**1917 British**, six lessons (`/?tutorial=basics|ranging|clockcode|heavy|counter|sos`):

- **The basics:** the screen, five units, camera, console, time compression; watch the 15-inch load, fire and ride its shell with F.
- **Ranging with the F.O.O.:** the 6-inch battery: take a mission, ranging round, telephone report, corrections, bracketing, fire for effect.
- **The aeroplane and the clock code:** the 9.2-inch: wireless messages and the RFC clock code.
- **The heavies:** the 15-inch with the delay fuze on a pillbox, then the railway gun on the railhead.
- **Counter-battery:** German fire walks onto you; HOLD; the 60-pounders on the sound rangers' reference.
- **S.O.S.:** red rockets, every gun on the line, shrapnel, break the attack.

**WW2 German**, six lessons (`/?mode=ww2&tutorial=w-basics|w-werfer|w-gustav|w-karl|w-v1|w-v2`; a lesson on another operation reloads the page there):

- **Die Grundlagen:** the leFH 18, metres and Strich.
- **Der Nebelwerfer**
- **Gustav: railway preparation**
- **Karl-Gerät**
- **V-1: the launch drill**
- **V-2: the A4 drill**

**WW1 German** has no lessons of its own; the 1917 and WW2 lessons teach the same drill. The Big Bertha demo has its own tutorial.
